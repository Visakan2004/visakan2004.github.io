DAY 01

Starting with the Microcontroller

Focus: Arduino Architecture & LED Circuits

The first day established the foundation for the rest of the week. I explored the Arduino board, its digital and power pins, basic microcontroller functionality, and how software instructions can directly control physical components.

Instead of code staying confined to screen buffers, instructions written in C++ were uploaded to the ATmega chip to toggle electrical states across breadboard rows.

DAY 02

Teaching Hardware to Sense

Focus: Sensors • DHT11 • IR • Ultrasonic • Serial Telemetry

Day 2 introduced the concept of giving a system awareness of its surroundings. I explored different types of sensors and learned how analog and digital physical phenomena can be converted into structured telemetry data that a microcontroller can process.

I interfaced sensors with the Arduino Uno and observed real-time telemetry streaming through the Serial Monitor. The DHT11 provided live temperature and relative humidity readings, while the IR and HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensors enabled proximity detection to trigger actuators such as servo motors.

DAY 03

Building Interaction with ESP32

Focus: ESP32 • OLED Display • Input Controls • Game Development

Day 3 moved beyond basic Arduino experiments into a significantly more capable embedded platform: the ESP32. I compared the ESP32 against the Arduino Uno and ESP8266, focusing on dual-core processing speeds, GPIO availability, integrated Wi-Fi/Bluetooth, and microcontroller architecture.

I connected an I2C OLED display and analog joystick controls to build a complete interactive computing experience. Instead of static text readouts, I developed and tested a custom falling-style game executed on the ESP32 with real-time collision detection, player state loops, and graphics rendering.

DAY 04

Connecting Hardware to the World

Focus: IoT • Wi-Fi • Smart Traffic • Wireless Web Servers

The final day introduced the most crucial transition of the week: from standalone hardware to connected hardware. Using the ESP32's integrated Wi-Fi stack, I bridged physical circuits with local and IP networks.

I hosted an embedded HTTP web server on the ESP32 (at 192.168.65.42), enabling browser-based toggles for individual GPIO channels (Blue on GPIO 26, Red on GPIO 27). I extended this into an automated IoT telemetry dashboard: when DHT11 temperature crossed 29.1°C, the dashboard triggered the red indicator lamp automatically while providing manual overrides.

System Architecture

03 — The Engineering Loop

The overarching insight from Week 04 is the complete embedded-system feedback cycle: from sensing the physical world to processing, actuating, connecting, and iteratively refining.

1. SENSE
Capture information from the physical environment through sensors.
2. PROCESS
Use programmed logic to interpret inputs and evaluate thresholds.
3. RESPOND
Drive physical outputs like LEDs, servos, motors, or OLED displays.
4. CONNECT
Extend hardware into connected digital networks via Wi-Fi and HTTP.
5. ITERATE
Test, identify edge cases, modify firmware, and optimize performance.

Conceptual Shift

05 — What Week 04 Changed

Week 04 helped me understand that embedded systems are not simply about connecting components. They are about creating a continuous relationship between the physical world and digital logic.

A sensor captures something happening around us. A microcontroller interprets it. An output responds. A network then connects that system to something beyond the device itself.

“IoT is not just about putting a device online. It is about connecting physical events, computation, decisions, and actions into one coherent system.”

“I started with a blinking LED and ended the week exploring connected systems — learning how hardware can sense, compute, respond, interact, and communicate.”

1. Laboratory Safety & Laser Operating Protocols

Operating industrial CO₂ laser systems requires strict adherence to Class 4 laser radiation safeguards, thermal fume management, high-voltage isolation, and optical cooling protocols. Prior to energizing the laser power supply, all machine and environmental interlocks were verified.

LASER RADIATION & ENCLOSURE

Operate only with protective chassis lid closed. The acrylic observation window blocks 10.6 μm infrared radiation. Interlock switches immediately terminate beam discharge if opened.

FUME EXTRACTION SYSTEM

Centrifugal exhaust ventilation must be active prior to laser emission to safely evacuate vaporized methyl methacrylate (MMA) fumes and airborne particles outdoors.

CLOSED-LOOP WATER CHILLER

Verified continuous distilled coolant flow through the sealed glass CO₂ tube at 18–22°C. Flow sensor interlock prevents thermal shock and tube fracture.

HIGH-VOLTAGE GROUNDING

Confirmed low-impedance electrical chassis grounding to dissipate static discharge and isolate 25–30 kV DC power supplies from sensitive DSP logic controllers.

COAXIAL AIR ASSIST

Constant coaxial air pressure delivered through the laser nozzle suppresses combustion flame-ups at the focal spot and shields the plano-convex lens from vapor deposits.

CONSTANT SUPERVISION

Maintain uninterrupted line-of-sight supervision during all active cutting passes. Clean honeycomb tray of flammable offcuts and ensure a CO₂ fire extinguisher is on standby.

3. Material Selection & Substrate Characteristics

Cast Acrylic (poly-methyl methacrylate — PMMA) was selected for its optical purity, structural rigidity, and clean vapor-phase degradation under 10.6 μm laser radiation. Unlike extruded acrylic or woods that char, cast PMMA directly depolymerizes into gaseous monomer, producing flame-polished edge walls and high-contrast frosted engraving.

Material Parameter Specification • Engineering Notes
Primary Material Cast Acrylic Sheet (Clear PMMA)
Calibrated Thickness 3.0 mm (verified uniformly across sheet with digital vernier caliper)
Processing Characteristics Clean sublimation without carbonization; produces flame-polished, optical-grade edges under 20.00 mm/s vector cut speed.
Surface Preparation Dual-sided protective craft paper masking preserved during raster engraving and cutting to prevent soot deposition and burn halos.
Raster Contrast Laser surface micro-fracturing produces high-contrast, frosted-white text and graphics against a transparent background.

5. Raster-to-Vector (DXF) Conversion Pipeline

The source raster artwork was vectorized through a structured image-processing pipeline to generate mathematically continuous Bézier paths compatible with CNC motion controllers.

1. SOURCE BITMAP 2. CONTRAST THRESHOLD 3. BÉZIER NODE DECIMATION 4. 50×50mm CALIBRATION 5. CLOSED DXF EXPORT
Kinematic Optimization via Spline Decimation

Automated edge tracers frequently generate thousands of redundant micro-nodes along smooth curves. In RuiDa DSP motion systems, dense nodes induce micro-acceleration transients and motor chatter. By smoothing Bézier control points into simplified mathematical curves while preserving 0.15 mm facial details, toolpath motion was rendered smooth and continuous.

7. Layer Hierarchy & Kinematics Configuration

The verified DXF geometry was positioned on the virtual coordinate table (X: 603.235 mm, Y: 407.214 mm) corresponding to available stock on the honeycomb bed. Toolpaths were divided into two prioritized processing layers.

Layer Colour Operation Speed Min Power Max Power Purpose • Path Priority
Layer 1 Black Laser Scan 100.00 mm/s 30.0% 30.0% High-contrast surface raster ablation of character faces and typography (Priority 1)
Layer 2 Blue Laser Cut 20.00 mm/s 60.0% 65.0% Through-cut internal 4.0 mm eyelet first, then outer 50×50 mm perimeter contour (Priority 2)
Critical Manufacturing Rule: Inside-to-Outside Cutting Sequence

In subtractive laser CAM, interior cutouts (the 4.0 mm lanyard hole) must always complete before outer perimeter separation. If the outer perimeter were cut first, the finished 50×50 mm piece would drop or vibrate on the honeycomb bed, resulting in eccentricity or failure when subsequently attempting to cut the eyelet.

9. Systematic Fabrication Workflow

The fabrication process followed a disciplined 5-stage standard operating procedure from optical calibration to final part extraction.

Stage 01 • Optics
1. Optical Datum Calibration Chiller and exhaust initiated. The 50.8 mm focal distance gauge block was placed between nozzle and acrylic stock to set optimal optical spot waist (~0.1 mm diameter).
Stage 02 • Stock
2. Stock Fixturing & Dry-Run Framing 3.0 mm cast PMMA sheet was secured flat on honeycomb bed. The RuiDa DSP controller executed a bounding box dry-run with red-dot laser to confirm coordinate bounds.
3. Toolpath Simulation RDWorks V8 toolpath simulation verified scanline hatch density, job cycle time (2m 18s), and confirmed eyelet cutting prior to perimeter separation.
4. Supervised Laser Execution Laser firing under active exhaust and coaxial air assist. Layer 1 raster engraving completed first, followed immediately by Layer 2 vector cut-through.
5. Dwell Cooling & Part Extraction Maintained 30-second dwell time for complete vapor evacuation. The finished 50×50 mm part released cleanly from surrounding stock without burrs.

11. Engineering Challenges & Root-Cause Mitigation

Observed Issue Root-Cause Analysis Engineering Resolution • Outcome
Thermal scorching and feature blooming on fine facial line art Excessive power duty cycle and thermal dwell on small acrylic surface caused localized re-melting. Modulated raster speed to 100.00 mm/s and restricted laser power to a rigid 30.0% ceiling on Layer 1 (Black). Yielded crisp high-definition silhouettes with zero thermal deformation.
Keyring eyelet eccentricity and kerf shift during through-cutting If the outer 50×50 mm boundary is severed first, the decoupled workpiece shifts on the honeycomb bed during the eyelet pass. Reordered CAM execution hierarchy in RDWorks to guarantee the internal 4.0 mm hole completes before outer perimeter separation. Delivered a true concentric circular bore.

12. Technical Reflection & Core Competencies

CAM LOGIC & HIERARCHY

Mastered dual-layer CAM architecture: decoupling high-speed surface raster engraving (100 mm/s @ 30%) from precision vector through-cutting (20 mm/s @ 65%), prioritizing interior features.

THERMAL DYNAMICS IN PMMA

Understood cast acrylic depolymerization dynamics under 10.6 μm CO₂ beams. Proper power-velocity balancing combined with coaxial air assist eliminates charring and yields optical edge clarity.

DSP HARDWARE CONTROL

Proficient in RuiDa RDC6442G controller operations: focal block datum setting, coordinate frame boundary verification, air-assist purging, and simulation telemetry validation.

PROCESS OPTIMIZATION

For industrial production batches, implement standardized power-speed matrix test coupons on scrap stock and evaluate reverse-side mirror engraving with acrylic paint infill.

Day 1

Understanding Users, Data & Better Solutions

“Week 1 began with a simple but important idea: strong products are built by understanding both user behaviour and real human problems.”

Recommendation Algorithms

“The first session introduced recommendation algorithms—the systems behind suggestions such as ‘Recommended for you,’ ‘Customers also bought,’ and personalised content feeds.”

User Affinity

Collaborative Filtering

Recommends items based on the interests and behaviour of users with similar preferences.

Feature Matching

Content-Based Filtering

Recommends items based on the features of content a user has previously liked or interacted with.

“Recommendation engines are not magic. They identify patterns in data and user behaviour to make digital experiences feel more relevant.”

“Netflix now knows me better than my own family. Concerning, but efficient.”

Design Thinking 101

“The next session introduced design thinking: a human-centred approach to solving problems.”

Human-Centred 5-Stage Process
Empathize Define Ideate Prototype Test

“Instead of rushing toward the first solution, design thinking encourages us to understand people, define the real problem, explore possibilities, test ideas, and improve through feedback.”

Key learning: “Fall in love with the problem, not your first idea.”

“Turns out the ‘obvious solution’ from minute one is rarely the right one. Rude, but fair.”

Eyewear Retail Case Study

“We applied the design thinking framework to an eyewear retail case study. As a team, we explored the customer journey and considered the challenges people may face while choosing, purchasing, or using eyewear.”

  • Understanding customer personas and needs
  • Identifying pain points in the buying experience
  • Brainstorming customer-focused solutions
  • Connecting ideas to practical user problems

“The activity showed us that the best ideas are not simply creative—they solve a genuine user need.”

“Four eyes are better than two — pun fully intended.”
Day 1 Takeaway
Data helps us understand patterns. Empathy helps us understand people. Great solutions need both.
Day 2

Turning Information Into Insight

“Day 2 focused on making better decisions: first through data visualization, and then through structured business analysis.”

Power BI Dashboards

“We explored how Power BI can transform large sets of spreadsheet data into dashboards that are easier to understand, explore, and act upon.”

  • Visualisations can make complex information easier to interpret
  • Filters help users explore data from different perspectives
  • Interactive dashboards turn reports into useful decision-making tools
  • Clear presentation matters as much as accurate data

“A good dashboard does not just show numbers. It helps people notice what matters.”

“Nothing says ‘I have my life together’ like a well-filtered pie chart.”

Design Thinking: The IDEO Method

“We also looked at IDEO’s approach to design thinking, with its emphasis on experimentation, iteration, collaboration, and learning through testing.”

“The process reminded us that progress often comes from trying, learning, improving, and trying again.”

“IDEO’s version of ‘fail fast’ sounds a lot more elegant than ours.”

Value Chain SWOT Analysis

“Our hands-on activity involved conducting a SWOT analysis across a value chain.”

  1. 1 Map the journey from raw input to the end customer
  2. 2 Study each stage individually
  3. 3 Identify strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats
  4. 4 Find gaps and prioritise areas for improvement

“Looking at the value chain stage by stage gave us a more practical understanding of where a business can improve.”

“SWOT: Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats — not Stressed, Worried, Overwhelmed, Tired.”
Day 2 Takeaway
Data is valuable when it is clear, and strategy is stronger when we look beyond the surface.

Day 4

Understanding Decisions & Building a Personal Brand

“Day 4 brought together two important ideas: understanding why people make decisions and learning how to communicate our own professional journey.”

Prospect Theory

“We explored prospect theory, a concept from behavioural economics that explains why people often feel losses more strongly than they value equivalent gains.”

The Core Insight

“People do not always make decisions based only on logic. Emotions, risk, fear of loss, and framing can strongly influence choices.”

“Understanding human behaviour is important for business, product design, communication, and everyday decision-making.”

“Explains perfectly why nobody in the group wanted to be the one who lost the case study debate.”

Building the Portfolio

“The portfolio-building session focused on personal branding and the importance of showing our skills, projects, interests, and growth in a clear and authentic way.”

“A portfolio is more than a collection of work. It is a story of how we think, what we care about, and how we solve problems.”

  • Showcase skills and learning
  • Document projects and experiences
  • Communicate clearly and authentically
  • Keep improving as confidence and experience grow
“Portfolio status: under construction, much like everyone’s confidence at 9am.”
Day 4 Takeaway
Understanding people helps us design better experiences; understanding ourselves helps us present our work with confidence.

Week 1 Key Takeaways

Seven core lessons from our first five days together.

  • 1 Technology is most useful when it solves a real human problem.
  • 2 Design thinking begins with empathy, not assumptions.
  • 3 Data becomes powerful when it is communicated clearly.
  • 4 Business strategy requires understanding the full value chain.
  • 5 Human behaviour influences decisions as much as logic does.
  • 6 A portfolio is an evolving record of learning and growth.
  • 7 Collaboration, iteration, and communication are essential professional skills.
